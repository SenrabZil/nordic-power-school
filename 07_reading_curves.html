A price line alone tells you little. Lay it beside demand, wind, and the balancing price, and the story writes itself — once you know which curve drives which.

01

The cast

Five curves, each on its own scale. The skill isn’t reading any one — it’s reading the relationships.

Day-ahead price — what cleared at the gate (€/MWh).
Imbalance price — real-time settlement (€/MWh).
Demand — the load to be served (GW).
Wind — near-zero-cost supply (GW).
Residual load — demand minus wind, the gap the stack must fill (GW).
02

What does price actually follow?

Demand peaks morning and evening; so does price — mostly. But look midday: demand barely dips, yet price falls off a cliff. Demand alone can’t explain it.

Predict first: which curve does the day-ahead price really track, hour for hour?

Scrub across the day and read down the cursor.

There it is: scale the residual-load line onto the price axis and it lies almost on top of price. Price follows the gap the dispatchable stack must fill — not raw demand. Wind is the wedge between them.

03

When two price curves split

Day-ahead and imbalance usually shadow each other — they’re the same energy, priced at two moments. Where they pull apart, real time didn’t match the plan.

Try it: scrub to 18:00. Imbalance towers over day-ahead — the evening ran short, so real-time energy cost far more. Now scrub to 12:00: imbalance sits below, the midday ran long. The gap between the two curves is a map of where the system was stressed.

04

How to read without fooling yourself

Mind the axes. These curves use different units. A crossing point means nothing unless they share a scale — align deliberately, or normalise.
Compare shape, not level. The insight is that residual and price move together, regardless of their absolute heights.
Watch the wedge. Demand minus wind is where the action is. When demand and price disagree, wind is almost always the reason.
Divergence is signal. Two curves splitting — day-ahead vs imbalance, zone vs zone — tells you something happened. Don’t smooth it away.
05

The rule, in one breath

Price follows residual load; the rest is read in the gaps between curves.

Lay the curves on a shared time axis, align scales honestly, and read shapes and divergences rather than levels. Demand minus wind is the driver; where day-ahead and imbalance part ways, the system was stressed. That is how a day becomes a story.
