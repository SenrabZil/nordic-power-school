Lesson 10 · Arbitrage

A spread is not
a profit.

Buy cheap, sell dear — but you sell less than you bought, and every cycle costs. The honest margin is what survives efficiency and wear.

with Elizabeth Cecilia. The trap is the day that looks profitable and isn’t. We’ll make the spread honest.

01

From spread to margin

Time-shifting energy looks simple: buy in the cheap hours, sell in the dear ones. But a store loses energy on the round trip — to deliver one MWh you must buy more than one — and each cycle wears it and pays fees. So the gross spread is only the starting point.

net = Psell − Pbuy / efficiency − cost

That division is the catch. At 85% efficiency you buy 1.18 MWh for every 1 you sell, so a low buy price still leaks value — and a thin spread can vanish entirely.

02

The efficiency bite

Take the volatile day from Lesson 08. A 4-hour cycle buys the cheapest four hours (~€23) and sells the dearest four (~€77) — a gross spread near €54.

Predict first: at 85% round-trip efficiency and a €4/MWh cycle cost, what’s the real per-MWh margin?

buy avg €23 (cheapest 4h) · sell avg €77 (dearest 4h) · gross €54

worth cycling net margin €46/MWh breakeven spread ≈ €31
Predict above to reveal the honest margin.

Even on a great day, efficiency and wear quietly skim the top. Drop efficiency or raise cost and watch the green shrink.

03

The day that isn’t worth it

The spread has to clear a hurdle: the efficiency leak plus the cost. On a flat day, it doesn’t.

Predict: switch the instrument to a calm day — same battery, same 85% and €4. Is a cycle still worth running?

A positive-looking spread isn’t a green light. Below the breakeven spread, the honest answer is: don’t cycle. That’s why batteries are choosy about which days — and which markets — they work.

This is the economics of where value comes from, not a dispatch schedule or a recommendation to trade. Real operation adds forecasting error, market rules, and risk the school doesn’t decide for you.

04

Reading arbitrage honestly

Divide, don’t subtract. Efficiency divides the buy price — the leak scales with what you pay to charge.
Spread must clear a hurdle. Below breakeven, a nominal profit is a real loss. Skip the day.
Depth has diminishing returns. Each extra cycle-hour reaches a narrower spread; eventually the marginal hour dips under breakeven.
Count every cost. Degradation, grid fees, taxes on charging — leave one out and the margin lies to you.
05

The rule, in one breath

Net margin is sell minus buy-over-efficiency minus cost — and only spreads above breakeven are worth a cycle.

Efficiency divides the buy price, wear and fees take the rest, and depth pays less the deeper you go. Plenty of days clear nothing once you’re honest.

Which is the whole reason for the next lesson: energy arbitrage alone rarely pays for a battery, so a battery earns from several markets at once — BESS value stacking.
