Lesson 14 · Virtual power plants

A thousand small things,
one power plant.

One home battery is too small to bid and too unreliable to trust. Pool a thousand of them and something firmer than any single asset emerges.

01

What a VPP is

A virtual power plant is software orchestrating many small, scattered resources — home batteries, EV chargers, heat pumps, flexible factories — so they act as one dispatchable plant. The aggregator sends signals, the assets respond, the market sees a single resource.

02

The market floor

Reserve and energy markets have a minimum bid size — often around a megawatt. A 5 kW home battery is two hundred times too small to enter on its own. Aggregate a thousand and you reach the floor; that’s the first reason VPPs exist. The second is firmness — and it’s the surprising one.

03

The portfolio effect

Each small asset is only sometimes available — the EV is unplugged, the battery is full, the factory is mid-run. Say any one is ready just 60% of the time. Individually, useless for a firm commitment.

Predict first: pool 1,000 such assets, each only 60% likely to be available. Of the capacity you’d expect on average, how much can you firmly promise the market?

expected 3.0 MW firmly commit 2.8 MW (92% of expected) clears the 1 MW floor

Drag the pool toward a handful of assets and the bell sprawls — you could end up with a fraction of what you promised. Drag it to thousands and it collapses to a spike: the spread shrinks with roughly the square root of the count. Flakiness becomes firmness.

04

The aggregator’s real job

The statistics are the easy part. Running a VPP is mostly the constraints around them.

Owner comfort comes first. The EV must be full by morning, the home kept warm. The VPP optimises around those limits — it doesn’t override them.
Proof of delivery is hard. You’re paid against a counterfactual baseline — what would have happened. For demand response, that baseline is genuinely tricky to measure.
Communications are the spine. Dispatching thousands of assets for fast reserves needs reliable, low-latency control and telemetry — or the firmness is only on paper.
The local grid still binds. These assets sit behind the meter on a distribution network; the DSO’s limits can cap what the fleet can actually deliver.

This explains how aggregation works in principle — not advice on operating a VPP, enrolling assets, or what to bid. Real portfolios live or die on contracts, comfort and network rules the school doesn’t decide.

05

The rule, in one breath

Aggregation turns many small, unreliable assets into one firm, market-ready plant.

Pooling clears the minimum-bid floor, and the portfolio effect shrinks the relative spread with the count — so a fleet of 60%-reliable assets can firmly commit nearly all its expected capacity. The hard part isn’t the maths; it’s comfort, measurement, comms and the grid.
