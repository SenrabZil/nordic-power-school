Lesson 18 · Intraday

Trading
the change.

The day-ahead auction clears once and freezes a plan. Then reality drifts — the wind comes in lighter, a unit trips — and the intraday market trades the difference.

with Elizabeth Cecilia. Day-ahead is the forecast; intraday is the correction.

01

One auction, then continuous

Around 13:00 CET the day-ahead auction sets a single price for every hour of tomorrow. But that price rests on forecasts — tomorrow’s wind, demand, plant availability. As those forecasts update through the day, the plan no longer matches reality. The intraday market runs continuously after the auction, right up to gate closure near delivery, so participants can trade away the difference between what they scheduled and what they now expect.

So intraday doesn’t re-price the whole day. It prices the change.

03

Why it moves, and which way

Less wind than the auction assumed means the system is suddenly short for those hours — something pricier has to cover the gap, so intraday prints above the day-ahead. Revise wind up and it’s the reverse: longer system, cheaper units, intraday below day-ahead, and on an already-soft hour it can chase toward zero. The intraday price is simply the day-ahead plus the correction the latest forecast demands.

Wind error is the dominant driver in the Nordics and across Europe — which is why intraday liquidity clusters around the hours and zones where wind matters most.

04

Gate closure and thinning liquidity

Two things move together as delivery nears: the forecast gets better (less to correct) and the order book gets thinner (fewer participants left to trade). So the largest, most tradeable corrections tend to come earlier, after a big forecast revision, while the last minutes before gate closure are sharp but shallow. Read intraday as the market’s running confession of how wrong yesterday’s plan turned out to be.

This explains what drives the intraday move — not advice on placing intraday orders or timing them. When and whether to trade the change is a decision the school doesn’t make.

05

The rule, in one breath

Day-ahead prices the plan; intraday prices the change to it.

When a forecast moves after the auction — wind above all — the system goes shorter or longer for those hours, and intraday steps away from the day-ahead by exactly the correction required. Less wind, prices up; more wind, prices down. Read the forecast revision and you’ve read the intraday move.
