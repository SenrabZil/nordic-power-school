01

Why reserves exist

Frequency is the grid’s heartbeat: 50 Hz means supply and demand match exactly, every instant. A unit trips, demand jumps — frequency moves at once, faster than any market can re-clear. So the TSO arranges reserves ahead of time: capacity standing ready to respond automatically.

Lesson 05 was the energy of balancing — what gets settled after. Ancillary services are the capacity arranged before: paid to be ready, whether or not they’re called.

02

The inertia problem

Spinning generators store kinetic energy — inertia — that slows how fast frequency can fall. Replace them with wind and solar (no spinning mass) and the same trip drops frequency faster and deeper.

Predict first: a large unit trips on a low-inertia hour, with no fast reserve. What does frequency do?

03

The fast fix

Inertia used to buy time for slower reserves. With less of it, the Nordics added Fast Frequency Reserve — activation in under a second — to catch the nadir before it reaches the load-shedding line.

Predict: flip FFR on at that same low-inertia trip. Does it save the nadir?

Slide inertia high and the dip is gentle — old grids barely needed FFR. Slide it low and FFR becomes the thing standing between a trip and the lights. That shift is why these markets are growing.

04

The reserve ladder

Reserves form a cascade by speed: the fast ones contain the fall, the slower ones restore frequency to 50 and free the fast ones up again.

FFR and FCR are automatic and near-instant; aFRR is automatic over minutes; mFRR is manually called to replace them. Together they hand the grid back to 50 Hz.

05

How they pay

Two revenue legs, and the first is the interesting one for a battery.

who

Fast assets win

Batteries respond in milliseconds, so they dominate FFR and FCR — built for exactly this.

This explains what these markets are and how they pay — it is not advice on which to bid into, or how. Procurement rules, qualification and risk are decisions the school doesn’t make for you.

06

The rule, in one breath

Ancillary services are capacity paid to stand ready, in a ladder of speed.

Less inertia means faster frequency swings, so the fast reserves — FFR, FCR — matter more every year. They pay mostly for availability, and batteries, responding instantly, are made for them.
