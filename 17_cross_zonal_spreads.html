Lesson 17 · Cross-zonal

Two prices,
one wire.

Neighbouring zones share a price only as far as the wire between them can carry. When the surplus outruns the cable, the border splits — and a spread opens you can read.

with Elizabeth Cecilia. This is Lesson 04’s split, watched across a whole day.

Builds on · 04 Zonal pricing · 08 Spreads & duration

01

The wire couples — until it can’t

Each bidding zone clears its own price. An interconnector lets cheap power flow from a surplus zone to a deficit one, pulling the two prices together — perfect coupling, one price, while there’s room on the cable. The moment the flow the market wants exceeds the cable’s capacity, the surplus is trapped: the exporting zone stays cheap, the importing zone stays dear, and a spread opens.

So the spread is the market’s asymmetry minus whatever the wire can carry away.

03

Capacity versus asymmetry

Slide the capacity. Build the cable bigger and the spread shrinks toward zero — the wire carries the whole asymmetry and the zones converge. Starve it and the spread widens and spreads across more hours. The border price difference is a tug-of-war: how unbalanced the two zones are, against how much the wire can move. Read both and you can anticipate the split before it prints.

04

The spread duration curve

Sort the day’s hourly spreads from widest to narrowest and you get the same duration curve idea from Lesson 08, now locational: not how high a price climbs, but how often a border is worth watching. A few wide hours, a long tail of coupling. Structural cuts — the classic Swedish SE2–SE3 north-to-south congestion, hydro and wind in the north, demand in the south — sit wide for many hours, year after year.

This explains how and when a spread opens — not advice on trading cross-zonal positions or transmission rights. Whether and how to act on a spread is a decision the school doesn’t make.

05

The rule, in one breath

The cross-zonal spread is the asymmetry the wire can’t carry.

Zones couple while the interconnector has room and split when the wanted flow exceeds it — exporter cheap, importer dear. Read the surplus-versus-deficit asymmetry against the capacity, and the spread’s width and its hours follow.
