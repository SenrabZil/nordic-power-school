Lesson 13 · Value stacking

One battery,
many revenues.

Arbitrage alone won’t pay for a battery. What makes it work is stacking — earning from the energy spread, from reserves, and from capacity, all in the same day.

with Elizabeth Cecilia. Everything from Lessons 08–12 converges here. The whole curriculum has been building this picture.

01

Why stack at all

Lesson 10 was blunt: many days clear little once efficiency and wear are honest. A battery living on the energy spread alone usually can’t cover its cost. So it earns from several streams at once — the spread, ancillary availability, occasional activation, and capacity payments.

But Lesson 12 set the limit: you can’t sell the same megawatt twice in the same hour. So stacking isn’t addition — it’s co-optimisation across streams and across the day.

03

Stacking across the day

The trick is time. Hold FCR through the quiet overnight hours when the spread is thin; swing into arbitrage for the evening peak when the spread is fat; keep capacity available throughout. One asset, different jobs at different hours.

State of charge ties it together: holding FCR needs mid-charge headroom, so the arbitrage swings have to fit around the reserve commitment. Co-optimisation is as much about the battery’s energy as its power.

04

Reading a stack honestly

Stacking is not addition. Streams share the same MW and MWh, so the total is less than each stream’s standalone best — co-optimised, not summed.
Headroom has a price. Reserving capacity for FCR forecloses arbitrage you might have done — count that opportunity cost.
Degradation spans all of it. Every cycle, from any stream, wears the cells. One wear budget across the whole stack.
Diversity is its own value. Several uncorrelated revenues are steadier than one — stacking lowers risk, not just raises the mean.

This shows how revenue streams combine in principle — not advice on what to build, bid, or stack, or whether any battery pays. Those depend on real costs, rules and risk the school doesn’t decide.

05

The rule, in one breath

A battery earns by stacking streams across the day, not by mastering one.

Arbitrage alone rarely clears the cost; reserves, activation and capacity stacked on top — co-optimised against finite power and energy — are what make it viable. Read the stack, not the headline of any single market.
