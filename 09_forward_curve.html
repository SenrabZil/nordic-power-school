Lesson 09 · Forwards

The market’s view,
months ahead.

Until now you read a single day. The forward curve reads the other direction — prices for delivery seasons away — and in the Nordics it’s shaped by weather and water.

with Elizabeth Cecilia. Two reads carry it: the seasonal wave, and the reservoir level that sets its height.

01

Two horizons

Day-ahead spot is one day’s clearing. Forwards (exchange-traded futures) are contracts for delivery weeks, months, or years out. Plot their prices against delivery period and you get the forward curve — the market’s best collective guess at future prices, the thing a producer or buyer uses to hedge.

It is a different product from spot — a different price_type — and it doesn’t move like spot. Where oil curves slope smoothly, a power curve is mostly a season.

02

The seasonal wave

Winter is dear: heating demand high, solar scarce, hydro reservoirs drawn down. Summer is cheap: snowmelt fills the reservoirs and demand falls. So the curve rises into each winter and sinks each summer — a wave, not a slope.

03

What sets its height: water

Hydro is half the Nordic system, so reservoir levels move the whole curve. Full reservoirs after a wet summer press forwards down; a dry autumn lifts them — and the lift is biggest where hydro is most stretched: winter.

Predict: a dry autumn leaves reservoirs well below normal. The coming winter’s forward, versus a normal year?

— watch the winter humps lift.

Slide reservoirs wet and the curve sinks, winter most of all. The level is fundamentals; the wave is the calendar. Read both at once.

04

Reading forwards honestly

Expectation, not promise. A forward is the market’s view plus a risk premium — it’s how risk is priced, not a guarantee of spot.
Compare like seasons. Winter-to-winter, summer-to-summer. Comparing a winter forward to a summer one tells you about the calendar, not the trend.
Mind the product. Futures are a separate price_type from day-ahead spot — and the Nordic venue itself changed in 2026. Tag the source.
Water is the level. When the whole curve shifts, look to reservoirs and hydrology before anything else.

Hedging is risk management, and reading the curve is market literacy — neither is a recommendation to buy, sell, or hedge anything. The school explains the mechanics; the decisions aren’t ours to make.

05

The rule, in one breath

A power forward curve is a seasonal wave on a fundamentals-set level.

Winter premium, summer discount — that’s the wave. Hydro reservoirs set how high it floats. Read it as expectation plus risk premium, compare like seasons, and tag it as the separate product it is.
