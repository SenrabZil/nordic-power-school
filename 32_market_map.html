Lesson 32 · Market structure

One auction, fifteen prices.

A single coupled auction runs the day-ahead market — yet the answer comes out different in NO1 than in LT. Zones, borders, cables and TSOs turn one European auction into many regional prices. Here is the map.

01

The bidding zones

One coupled auction runs the Nordic and Baltic day-ahead market — yet the cleared price can differ from one area to the next. The market is carved into bidding zones, and each zone can hold its own price when the wires between them fill up.

The Nordic and Baltic bidding zones, wired by interconnectors, with the faint links out to GB, DE and PL. A schematic, not a survey map.

Predict: how many bidding zones cover the Nordics and the Baltics together?

Norway 5, Sweden 4, Finland 1, Denmark 2, plus Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania — fifteen in all. Lesson 04 shows why a single price splits along these borders.
02

Who runs each wire

Every zone sits inside a national grid run by a Transmission System Operator. They operate the system, publish much of the open data this school reads, and procure the reserves that keep it balanced.

03

Marketplace and settlement

Trading and settlement are different institutions. Nord Pool is the exchange where the day-ahead and intraday markets run, coupled to the rest of Europe (SDAC for day-ahead, SIDC for intraday). eSett is the central body that settles imbalances across the Nordics — it never trades; it nets what each BRP owes or is owed.

Predict: when your portfolio ends a delivery hour out of balance, who settles the bill?

Trade on the exchange; balance with the TSO in real time; settle the deviation through eSett. Three institutions, three jobs.
04

The wires out

The region is not an island. High-voltage links reach Germany, Poland, the Netherlands and Great Britain, and the day-ahead auction allocates them implicitly — cheap power flows toward dear, until the cable fills. That is why a border is not administrative trivia: it is a price engine. When a wire saturates, the price splits across it; when it has room, the two sides converge.

Lesson 04 (zonal pricing) and Lesson 21 (interconnectors & coupling) take this apart in detail; Lesson 26 covers the Baltic corner and its 2025 switch to continental synchronisation.

05

The rule, in one breath

Prices are local because the grid is local.

Zones, borders, cables and TSOs turn one European auction into many regional prices. Read a Nordic price and you are really reading a zone, the wires around it, and how full they are.
