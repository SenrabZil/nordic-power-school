Lesson 04 · Zones

When the wire
fills, price splits.

Lesson 03 found one price. But the Nordics are a dozen zones stitched by interconnectors — and when a wire saturates, one price becomes two.

with Elizabeth Cecilia. The split isn’t a glitch in the data. It’s the grid telling you where the constraint is.

01

Coupling: cheap power flows to dear

Each zone runs its own auction with its own stack and demand. Left alone, a windy northern zone clears cheap and a high-demand southern zone clears dear. Connect them and power flows from cheap to dear — the north exports until the two prices meet in the middle. Market coupling hands you one price across both.

But the interconnector has a limit. If the flow needed to equalise them exceeds the wire’s capacity, the wire saturates — and the prices can’t finish converging.

Now starve the wire

The wire pins at its limit, the north keeps its cheap surplus and the south is stuck importing too little — so the south price stays high. The gap between them, times the flow, is the congestion rent the TSOs collect. Push capacity to zero and you’re back to two isolated auctions.

A north–south spread like this is routine in Sweden. It is not an error to clean away; it is the signal. Comparing zones without knowing which are coupled will mislead you every time.

05

The rule, in one breath

One price when the wires are ample; a split when they congest.

Power flows cheap-to-dear until prices meet or the interconnector saturates. A saturated link holds the spread open, and that spread times the flow is the congestion rent. Zonal price differences are a signal about the grid, never a defect in the feed.
