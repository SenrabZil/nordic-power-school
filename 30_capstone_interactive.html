One Hour, End to End

You've learned each market on its own. Day-ahead, intraday, balancing, settlement. Now watch them be one thing: a single delivery hour, a single position, carried all the way through — and a final number that depends on every stage before it.

LEVEL · CAPSTONE PREREQUISITES · ALL FOUR LESSONS MENTOR · ELIZABETH CECILIA ~30 MIN
Education only

A teaching walk-through, not a strategy. The whole point of this episode is that the "right" choice depends on an outcome you couldn't know in advance — so nothing here is advice. Every figure is synthetic and deterministic.

The setup

One battery, one hour, the whole timeline

You operate a 50 MW battery in Finland. We're going to follow delivery hour 18 tomorrow from the moment the day-ahead auction clears to the moment the last cent is settled — making one decision along the way and watching it ripple all the way to the end.

Use the episode panel below as your spine: step it forward stage by stage as you work through each beat. Read the driver, commit, and see where your position lands.

STAGE 01 — DAY-AHEAD

The plan clears, and a gap opens

The day-ahead auction clears hour 18 at €48/MWh. You sell 50 MWh into it, booking +€2,400. But when you check your battery's state of charge for that hour, you can only physically deliver 30 MWh. You're 20 MWh short of what you just sold.

Predict — pick one

You sold 50 but can deliver 30. Is your day-ahead revenue at risk?

The reasoning

The day-ahead sale is done — €2,400 is yours at €48, whatever happens next. What's now open is a physical gap: 20 MWh you're committed to deliver but can't. That shortfall has to be sourced somewhere downstream — and where you source it, and at what price, is the whole rest of this episode.

Step the panel to Intraday and let's price the first option.

STAGE 02 — INTRADAY

News breaks. You can close the gap now — or not.

At 13:00 the wind forecast for hour 18 drops 2 GW. You've seen this film: intraday for that hour firms. It's now €58/MWh. You can buy your 20 MWh short here and now for −€1,160, squaring your position — or carry the short into delivery and take whatever real time hands you.

Predict — pick one

After a 2 GW wind drop, which way did intraday for hour 18 move?

The reasoning

It firmed to €58 — above your €48 sale. So covering the short now locks a known cost of €1,160, but at a worse price than you sold at. Carrying it leaves the cost open, to be set by whatever the balancing stage does. There's no free option here; there's a known cost now versus an unknown cost later.

Make your call in the panel — cover or carry — then step to Balancing.

STAGE 03 — BALANCING

Real time decides the price you didn't lock

Delivery arrives. Wind came in even lower than intraday had priced; the system is short, the TSO activates up-regulation, and the 15-minute imbalance price settles at €95/MWh — well above both your sale and the intraday level.

Predict — pick one

A short system on a low-wind evening — where does the imbalance price sit?

The reasoning

Short system, up-regulation, €95. If you carried your 20 MWh short, this is the price it now settles at — €1,900 out the door, far worse than the €1,160 covering would have cost. If you covered, you're square and this price never touches you. Step to Settlement and total it up.

STAGE 04 — SETTLEMENT

The number — and why it nearly went the other way

Now the tally. But before you read it, the real capstone question isn't what the number is — it's whether the choice that produced it was the right one, and whether it would be right again.

Predict — pick one

For this episode, which path nets more — and would it hold next time?

The reasoning

For this episode, covering wins: +€1,240 against carrying's +€500. But flip the contingency toggle in the panel — had the system ended long, the imbalance price would have fallen to around €10, and carrying would have netted +€2,200, beating covering handily.

Same hour, same position, same decision points — and the verdict reverses entirely on something you could not know when you had to choose: which way the system would end. That's the lesson the whole school has been building toward. There's no rule that says "always cover" or "always carry." There's only a connected system in which the price you don't lock rolls forward to the next stage, and the imbalance backstop prices whatever's left — at a level the system sets, not you.

The whole arc

What you've actually learned

  • Day-ahead locks revenue; physics opens exposure. The sale was safe; the 20 MWh you couldn't deliver was the real story.
  • Every market reprices the same MWh. €48 day-ahead, €58 intraday, €95 (or €10) at the backstop — one position, valued four times.
  • What you don't close rolls forward. The gap left open at each stage lands at the next, until the imbalance backstop catches whatever remains.
  • Cover versus carry has no universal answer. This episode rewarded covering; a long system would have rewarded carrying. The choice is made under uncertainty, always.
  • The four markets are one system. Every lesson in this school is a magnified view of a single stage of this one timeline.

That's the arc — day-ahead to settlement, one hour, one position. You can run the episode again and make the other choice, or flip the system, and watch the whole chain rearrange itself. Then go back to any single lesson and you'll see it differently: not a market on its own, but one link in this.

The rule, in one breathDay-ahead locks revenue, every later market reprices what’s left, and the imbalance backstop catches the rest — at a price the system sets, contingent on which way it broke.
