Lesson 31 · Market structure

The market is a stack of jobs.

“The power market” is not one thing. Between the turbine and your meter sits a chain of roles, each answerable for a different slice. Learn the stack, and every later lesson has somewhere to sit.

with Elizabeth Cecilia, your mentor. When I ask you to predict, actually predict — before you reveal.

01

Two stories at once

“The power market” is really two stories told over the same wire. One is physical: electrons leave a generator, cross the transmission grid, step down through distribution, and reach a meter. The other is commercial: a plan is sold, matched and settled in money — and someone is held responsible when reality drifts from the plan.

The same company can be a generator (top) and a trader (bottom) at once — the asset is physical, the responsibility is commercial.

Predict: a wind farm sells 100 MWh in the day-ahead auction. The wind drops; it makes only 80. Whose problem is the missing 20?

Every generator and trader sits behind a Balance Responsible Party. The TSO acts physically in real time; the BRP pays for the gap afterwards.
02

The role stack, named

Hold these apart and the rest of the course stops being a soup of acronyms. Each role owns one job.

operates the grid

TSO

Transmission System Operator (Fingrid, Statnett…). Runs the high-voltage grid and keeps the system balanced in real time.

operates the grid

DSO

Distribution System Operator. Runs the local low-voltage network that reaches homes and businesses.

carries the plan

BRP

Balance Responsible Party. Financially answerable for the difference between what its portfolio planned and what it actually did.

sells flexibility

BSP

Balancing Service Provider. Offers reserves — up or down regulation — into the balancing markets.

makes the MW

Generator

Owns and runs production: hydro, wind, nuclear, thermal, batteries.

trades the plan

Trader

Buys and sells energy across day-ahead, intraday and forwards to shape a position.

sells to end users

Supplier

Buys wholesale, sells a retail contract, and bills the consumer.

pools the small

Aggregator

Bundles many small assets or loads into one market-ready unit (see VPPs).

matches & clears

Exchange

Nord Pool and the clearing house: where bids meet and trades are guaranteed.

settles the money

eSett

The Nordic imbalance settlement body — nets every BRP’s deviation into a bill.

03

The same megawatt, two paths

A single megawatt-hour travels two routes that never quite line up. Physically it flows wherever the laws of the grid send it — you cannot label an electron. Commercially it is wrapped in contracts: who sold it, who is responsible for it, who pays for it. Confusing the two is the source of most beginner errors: a cable does not “carry your trade”, and your contract does not move an electron.

When a lesson later says “Finland imported from Sweden,” it means the physical flow. When it says “the BRP was short,” it means the commercial story. Keep the two ledgers separate.

04

Why it is many markets

There is no single “power market.” A delivery hour passes through a sequence of venues — forwards, day-ahead, intraday, balancing — and is then settled. Each is a separate process; the BRP is the thread that runs through all of them.

Predict: for a given delivery hour, which of these clears first?

Order of events: forwards (months out) → day-ahead (D−1) → intraday (up to delivery) → balancing (real time) → settlement (after). Lesson 33 walks this whole arc.
05

The rule, in one breath

Name the role, and the rest follows.

Someone owns the asset, someone trades the plan, someone balances the deviation, someone operates the grid, and someone settles the money. Every later lesson — eSett, BRPs, reserve markets, imbalance settlement, VPPs — is just one of these roles, doing its one job.
