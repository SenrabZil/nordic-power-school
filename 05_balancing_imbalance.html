Lesson 05 · Balancing

After the gate,
the grid still has
to balance.

Day-ahead set a plan. Real time never matches it — so the TSO trims the difference, and whoever drifted from plan is settled at one price.

with Elizabeth Cecilia. The twist worth learning: that single price can pay you for drifting — or punish you — depending on which way the system needed you to go.

01

Real time never matches the plan

The grid must hold 50 Hz every second. When consumption and generation drift apart, the system goes short (under-generating) or long (over-generating), and the TSO activates reserves: up-regulation to make more when short, down-regulation to back off when long.

The marginal activated reserve sets the balancing price — merit order again, but for adjustments. And the parties who deviated from their day-ahead schedule are settled at a single imbalance price derived from it.

03

Your settlement, at that one price

Here is the part that catches people out. Your imbalance is settled at the single system price, whichever way you went. So the same megawatt-hour is worth wildly different things depending on what the system needed.

Predict: tight evening — system short, imbalance price €110. Your plant over-produced, so you have surplus to sell. At what price do you sell it?

This explains how settlement works — it is not a suggestion to run imbalances on purpose. Staying on your schedule is the job; the imbalance price is what you face when you can’t.

04

Where to read it: Fingrid

In Finland these prices come from Fingrid’s Open Data — UTC in and out, distinct datasets you tag by price_type so a balancing price is never mistaken for day-ahead spot.

dataset 92

Consumption imbalance price

The settlement price for consumption-side imbalance.

dataset 93

Production imbalance price

The production-side counterpart.

dataset 251

Down-regulation price

The real-time price of the last activated down-regulation bid.

Mind one trap: a balancing or imbalance value can be indicative before it’s final. Record which dataset and the capture time in provenance, so an early number is never read as a settled one.

05

The rule, in one breath

The system’s direction sets one imbalance price; your deviation meets it head-on.

Short systems pull the price up, long systems push it down or negative. Deviate the way the system needed and the single price rewards you; deviate against it and the same price penalises you. It is a different price_type from day-ahead — tag it, and treat indicative values as provisional.
