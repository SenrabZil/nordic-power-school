Builds on · 04 Zonal pricing

01

The implicit auction

Once, traders bought interconnector capacity in a separate auction, then decided what to send — explicit allocation, and easy to get wrong. Today it’s implicit: the day-ahead auction clears every coupled zone as a single optimisation, and the algorithm allocates the cable itself, sending power from the low-price side to the high-price side up to the capacity available. You bid in your own zone; the coupling does the rest, across all of Europe’s linked markets at once.

So the cable isn’t something you use. It’s something the market solves.

03

Who earns the gap

When the cable saturates and a spread survives, that spread isn’t lost — it becomes congestion rent: the price difference times the flow, collected by the TSOs and put toward grid and interconnector investment. Notice the twist in the readout: a full outage gives the widest gap but zero rent — because nothing flows. Rent needs both a spread and a flow; the most congested healthy cable, not the broken one, earns the most.

04

How much capacity even reaches the market

The number on that slider isn’t the cable’s physical rating — it’s what the TSO gives the market after holding back margin for security. Two ways to compute it: NTC (a simple fixed limit per border, the Nordic tradition) and flow-based (a richer model that captures how a flow on one border loads the whole meshed grid, and typically frees up more capacity). The Nordics have been moving from NTC toward flow-based coupling — same auction, a smarter view of what the wires can really carry.

NTC / ATC. One number per border. Simple, but conservative on a meshed network — it can’t see loop flows.
Flow-based. Constraints across the whole grid at once. More capacity offered, prices that reflect the physics more faithfully.
05

Cables are assets, and they trip

Each interconnector is a physical thing — the HVDC links with names: NordLink to Germany, NorNed to the Netherlands, Estlink to Estonia. When one goes on outage, planned or not, its capacity vanishes from the auction and the zones it joined re-split, the surplus trapped on the cheap side. Outages are published ahead (the REMIT transparency notices), so a scheduled cable maintenance is a price event you can see coming — one of the most readable signals on the board.

This explains how coupling allocates capacity and how outages move prices — not advice on trading interconnector spreads or transmission rights. What to do with a coupling signal is a decision the school doesn’t make.

06

The rule, in one breath

The auction allocates the cable implicitly, flowing low to high until prices meet or the wire saturates.

You bid in your zone; the coupling sends power across borders and converges prices wherever capacity allows. The surviving spread becomes congestion rent — gap times flow — and the capacity offered depends on NTC vs flow-based. When a cable trips, its zones re-split. Read the wire and the outages, and you read the borders.
